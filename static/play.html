How it works

Our Song is a game for the jukebox. Two people, six cards each, one song they would both play, chosen on Qloo's numbers, not a guess.

101 cards, four suits

Stage (what you turn up), Bar (what you order), Road (what you do with a Saturday), Porch (who you are at home). Every card stands for one Qloo signal, an audience or a tag, checked against the live API before it was allowed in. Cards whose signal returned noise were left out. The joker, On the house, has no signal: play it and a song you both say yes to is free.

See the deck

The engine

Before any game, Qloo scored every card against 2,003 artists bars actually play: a matrix of 101 columns. Each column is standardized, so a life card and a genre card pull by rank, not by how wide Qloo's numbers run. Your three cards weigh 3, 2, 1; the cards you tossed pull against. Each of you gets a curved score for every artist, and the weaker score decides, so nobody gets dragged. The top three are checked live, Qloo's compare says what you share, and the song's top tracks come from the catalog.

The bar

Tonight: the venue. The venue owns a line on the table and one while Q-Lou thinks. A song costs four tokens: both say yes and it is two each; a no brings the next pick, three in all; after the third, nobody pays. The last 25 picks at the venue sit out of the draft, so regulars do not hear the same song twice.

The venue screen shows the table on the bar's TV.

Why trust it

Every number on screen is Qloo's. Beside each pick, a plain language model makes its own guess from the same cards, with no data, and Qloo scores that guess too. You see both. Q-Lou's lines come from a file, not a model.

Open this page on two phones and join with the room code to play for real. The demo seats you with a house partner who plays on a human clock.
